# RESEARCH_METHOD

## §1 Purpose

RESEARCH_METHOD defines how research enters Atlas. It governs research quality, not research topics. It does not choose what to investigate; it defines what an investigation must produce to be admitted into Atlas and what an Atlas entry must carry to remain trustworthy over time.

This document is descriptive of the research process, not of the research itself. Amendments to this document affect every future Atlas entry and every review of past entries. Existing entries are not retroactively invalidated by an amendment; they carry the method version under which they were admitted (see §7).

The Company Reference is the authoritative document. RESEARCH_METHOD serves Atlas, and Atlas serves the Company Reference through the amendment interface defined in §6. Research never updates the Company Reference directly. Only a formal amendment through the process defined in the Company Reference can change the Company Reference.

## §2 First Principles

The following principles govern every act of research the company conducts, sponsors, or admits into Atlas. Amendment of any First Principle requires the formal amendment process defined elsewhere.

**FP1 — Research seeks truth, not consensus.** The goal is to find what is, not what most researchers currently claim. A widely-held claim is a datum, not a verdict.

**FP2 — Consensus is evidence, never proof.** A consensus finding enters Atlas as one entry with its own confidence label. It does not gain automatic authority from its wide acceptance.

**FP3 — Minority evidence is preserved.** A finding that runs against the dominant view is retained in Atlas with the same care as a majority finding. Suppression of minority evidence is a research failure.

**FP4 — Contradictory evidence is preserved.** When two findings disagree, both remain in Atlas. Neither is silently reconciled and neither is deleted. Contradictions are handled per §4.

**FP5 — Personal experience is admissible.** First-person observation enters Atlas when it is dated, documented with the observation method, and reproducible in the sense that another observer following the same protocol could produce a comparable account. Undocumented anecdote is not admissible.

**FP6 — Expert opinion is evidence, not authority.** An expert's claim enters Atlas as a source-attributed datum with a confidence label proportional to the evidence the expert has produced, not proportional to the expert's standing.

**FP7 — Mechanism outranks correlation.** A finding that explains why a phenomenon occurs is weighted above a finding that merely observes that the phenomenon co-occurs with another. Correlation without mechanism enters Atlas at a smaller confidence than mechanism with corroborating correlation.

**FP8 — Replication outranks novelty.** A finding that has been independently reproduced is weighted above a novel finding that has not. Novelty is not a virtue in research; correspondence with reality is.

**FP9 — Unknown is a valid conclusion.** An investigation that ends without a finding produces an Atlas entry labeled Unknown. This entry has full standing.

**FP10 — Insufficient evidence is a valid outcome.** An investigation that ends with evidence too weak to support a finding produces an Atlas entry labeled Insufficient. This entry has full standing.

## §3 Evidence Hierarchy

Evidence Hierarchy ranks the kinds of evidence admissible to Atlas. The rank governs the confidence label an entry can carry (§5). It does not govern which topics the company investigates.

**E1 — Mechanistic evidence with independent replication.** A finding for which the underlying mechanism is understood and which has been reproduced by parties independent of the original source.

**E2 — Independent replication without settled mechanism.** A finding that has been reproduced by independent parties but whose mechanism remains unexplained.

**E3 — Single well-conducted investigation with reproducible method.** A finding produced by one investigation whose method is fully documented and admits replication.

**E4 — Documented first-person observation.** A single observer's account, dated, with method recorded, and reproducible in the sense of FP5.

**E5 — Contested evidence.** Evidence that another Atlas entry contradicts. Contested evidence remains in Atlas at its original tier, with its contradiction cross-referenced.

Every entry retains its own tier. When multiple entries bear on the same question, all remain in Atlas at their respective tiers. Confidence labeling per §5 accounts for the full set of entries bearing on the question.

Undocumented anecdote, unreproducible experience, and appeal-to-authority claims without underlying evidence are not admissible at any tier.

## §4 Contradiction Protocol

When two Atlas entries disagree on the same question, both remain in Atlas. Reconciliation is not the default; coexistence is.

**Step 1 — Recognize.** The contradiction is recorded as its own Atlas entry: what each of the disagreeing entries claims, on what question they disagree, and the date the disagreement was noticed.

**Step 2 — Cross-label.** Each contradicting entry is labeled with a cross-reference to the other. Neither entry is silently modified.

**Step 3 — Preserve.** Neither entry is deleted, moved to an archive that hides it from search, or downgraded in confidence solely on the basis of being contradicted.

**Step 4 — Investigate.** The contradiction opens a new Atlas entry in the Open Questions register (Atlas Part V). The investigation is dated and follows the same evidence standards as any other research.

**Step 5 — Resolve, if evidence supports resolution.** A resolution enters Atlas as a new entry that supersedes the disagreeing entries. Superseded entries remain visible and are marked as superseded, with a cross-reference to the resolving entry.

**Step 6 — Accept unresolved contradiction as a valid state.** When evidence does not support resolution, both entries continue to coexist. The Open Question that captures the contradiction remains open.

Minority evidence is protected under this protocol. A single dissenting entry against a large body of agreeing entries is not deleted, hidden, or automatically downgraded. It stands as a labeled minority entry.

## §5 Confidence Framework

Every Atlas entry carries a confidence label. The label reflects the current evidence supporting the entry, not the entry's importance or the researcher's investment in it.

**C1 — Established.** Evidence at tier E1, corroborated by additional E1 or E2 evidence, with no active contradiction in Atlas.

**C2 — Corroborated.** Evidence at tier E1 or E2, with supporting evidence in Atlas and no active contradiction.

**C3 — Supported.** Evidence at tier E3 with a documented method, or evidence at E1 or E2 with a currently unresolved contradiction.

**C4 — Preliminary.** Evidence at tier E4, or a single E3 entry without replication.

**C5 — Contested.** Evidence at any tier where an active contradiction is unresolved per §4.

**Unknown.** The entry records that the question was investigated and no admissible evidence was produced.

**Insufficient.** The entry records that the evidence produced was too weak to support any conclusion.

Confidence is reassessed whenever a new entry bears on the same question. Reassessment records the date, the new evidence, and the resulting label change. Prior labels are preserved in the entry's history.

## §6 Decision Threshold

Atlas evidence does not update the Company Reference directly. Only a formal amendment through the process defined in the Company Reference can change the Company Reference. This Section defines the evidence threshold at which an Atlas finding warrants surfacing to the amendment process.

**Threshold for surfacing.** An Atlas entry is surfaced to the Company Reference custodian when all of the following hold:

- The entry bears on a Company Reference position — a value, a principle, an assumption, an ethical boundary, a category claim, or a Success Criterion.
- The entry carries a confidence label of C1 or C2, OR the entry is C5 (Contested) with the contradiction bearing directly on a Company Reference position.
- The entry has been in Atlas for a minimum review period defined by the amendment process. Very recent findings do not trigger surfacing until they have been available for review.

**Threshold for amendment.** Surfacing is a request for review, not a decision. The amendment process decides whether the entry warrants a change to the Company Reference. The amendment process can:

- Amend the position in light of the entry.
- Retain the position, recording the entry as considered.
- Retain the position pending additional evidence, and open an Atlas Open Question to track the additional evidence.
- Amend a related position while retaining the primary position.

**Threshold for no action.** Entries at C3, C4, or C5 where the contradiction does not bear on a position remain in Atlas without surfacing. They contribute to future surfacing when strengthened by additional evidence.

Amendments to the Company Reference cite the Atlas entry ID that motivated them. The reverse link is not maintained by Atlas; the Company Reference is downstream of Atlas in the flow, not the other way around.

## §7 Research Output Format

Every entry admitted to Atlas carries the following fields. Entries missing any field are not admissible. Entries are not admitted "provisionally" or "pending correction." The full field set at admission is what future readers will see.

**Identifier.** A permanent, immutable Atlas entry ID. Never reassigned. Cited by the Company Reference and by other Atlas entries.

**Date.** The date the entry was admitted to Atlas.

**Author.** The researcher who admitted the entry. If multiple, the primary author is named and additional contributors are listed.

**Question.** The specific research question the entry addresses. Written as a question, not as a topic.

**Claim.** The finding the entry records. Written as a single, testable statement. Where the entry records Unknown or Insufficient per FP9 or FP10, the claim field records the outcome literally.

**Source.** The source or sources from which the claim is drawn. Every source carries a permanent reference that a future reader can retrieve.

**Method.** The method by which the claim was produced. Where the entry cites external research, the external method is summarized and referenced. Where the entry records original investigation, the method is documented in full.

**Evidence Tier.** The tier per §3 (E1 through E5).

**Confidence Label.** The label per §5 (C1 through C5, Unknown, or Insufficient).

**Rationale.** The explicit reasoning that connects the source and method to the claim. A reader disagreeing with the claim can locate the specific step in the rationale to disagree with.

**Contradictions.** Every Atlas entry that contradicts this one, referenced by Atlas ID. When no contradiction is known, the field explicitly records "None known at time of entry."

**Open Questions.** Questions the claim raises that remain unresolved. Each is cross-referenced to its entry in Atlas Part V. New entries open new Open Questions where appropriate.

**Supersession Status.** The entry is either current (the working version) or superseded. Superseded entries record the Atlas ID of the superseding entry. Every version remains readable.

**Method Version.** The version of RESEARCH_METHOD under which the entry was admitted. Amendments to RESEARCH_METHOD do not retroactively invalidate entries admitted under earlier versions.

An entry that carries every field but fails the standards defined in §3 or §5 is not admitted.
