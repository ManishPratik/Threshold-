# ATLAS — Human Understanding Repository

## Version 1.0 — Skeleton

---

## Part I — Foundations

### 1.1 Scope
*Purpose: define the domain Atlas covers and the domain Atlas does not cover. Atlas holds descriptive evidence about the human phenomena the company addresses; it does not hold company decisions, product specifications, or engineering artefacts. This Part fixes the outer boundary of what belongs here.*

### 1.2 Research Method
*Purpose: state the methods by which evidence is admitted into Atlas — observation protocols, interview protocols, literature-review protocols, experimental protocols. Method is fixed here so that entries across decades remain comparable and reviewable.*

### 1.3 Evidence Standards
*Purpose: define what qualifies as evidence in Atlas — what an entry must contain to be admissible, how sources are cited, how raw data is retained, and how anecdotes are distinguished from findings. Nothing enters Atlas without meeting the standard.*

### 1.4 Confidence Levels
*Purpose: define the confidence labels applied to every entry — the categories of certainty Atlas recognizes and the criteria that determine an entry's label. Confidence is a first-class property of every finding.*

### 1.5 Contradictions
*Purpose: define how contradictory evidence is handled — when two entries disagree, both remain in Atlas, both are labeled, and neither is silently reconciled. This Part governs the coexistence of competing findings.*

### 1.6 Decision Interface
*Purpose: define how Atlas entries are surfaced to the Company Reference and to downstream decisions — how a finding is cited by an amendment, how an Atlas entry ID is referenced, and how the one-way flow from Atlas to Company Reference is maintained.*

## Part II — Human Understanding

### 2.1 Human Nature
*Purpose: hold evidence about the species-level constants that shape every human — the durable architecture of cognition, drive, and constraint that persists across cultures and eras.*

### 2.2 Motivation
*Purpose: hold evidence about what moves a human to act — the sources, structures, and dynamics of motivation as they present in the domain the company addresses.*

### 2.3 Identity
*Purpose: hold evidence about how a human constructs, maintains, and revises the sense of who they are — including the relationship between identity and commitment.*

### 2.4 Habits
*Purpose: hold evidence about repeated behavior — how habits form, how they persist, how they weaken, and how they interact with commitment and intention.*

### 2.5 Addiction
*Purpose: hold evidence about compulsive behavior the person did not choose or no longer wishes to continue — including recovery, relapse, and the mechanisms that support cessation.*

### 2.6 Emotion
*Purpose: hold evidence about the affective states that accompany commitment, keeping, breaking, and self-observation.*

### 2.7 Attention
*Purpose: hold evidence about the human capacity to attend — its scarcity, its allocation, its interruption, and its recovery.*

### 2.8 Decision Making
*Purpose: hold evidence about how a human chooses — including the deliberate, the automatic, the deferred, and the abandoned choice.*

### 2.9 Self-Trust
*Purpose: hold evidence about the phenomenon at the center of the company's Purpose — how self-trust is built, sustained, eroded, and reconstructed.*

### 2.10 Behavior Change
*Purpose: hold evidence about the process by which a human alters what they do — the conditions that accelerate change, the conditions that stall it, and the conditions that reverse it.*

### 2.11 Performance
*Purpose: hold evidence about the sustained capacity to act well over time — including the interaction between commitment, capacity, and the constraints that limit both.*

### 2.12 Relationships
*Purpose: hold evidence about the human's relational field as it bears on self-commitment — including how other people affect a person's ability to keep their word to themselves.*

### 2.13 Meaning
*Purpose: hold evidence about the human's construction of purpose — what makes a commitment feel worth keeping and what makes it feel hollow.*

### 2.14 Suffering
*Purpose: hold evidence about the shapes of human pain that intersect the domain the company addresses — including the pain of self-broken promises and the pain of the mechanism that records them.*

### 2.15 Flourishing
*Purpose: hold evidence about what it is for a human to live well — including the enduring conditions that support flourishing across time.*

---

## Part III — Evidence Library
*Purpose: catalogue every primary source Atlas draws on — studies, papers, books, interviews, field notes, observational reports. Each source has a permanent identifier, a summary of its methodology, and pointers to the Atlas entries that cite it.*

---

## Part IV — Experiments
*Purpose: record every experiment the company has conducted or observed — hypothesis, method, conditions, outcome, and interpretation. Experiments are dated, versioned, and preserved regardless of outcome.*

---

## Part V — Open Questions
*Purpose: hold the current state of investigation for every enduring research direction the company has committed to pursue (as fixed in Company Reference Part III §3.4) and for every open question that emerges from Atlas evidence itself. Questions are added; questions are not closed.*

---

## Part VI — Decision History
*Purpose: preserve the historical record of every Atlas amendment — added entries, superseded entries, re-classified entries, and re-labelled entries. Nothing is deleted; the shape of understanding over time is itself part of the corpus.*

---

## Governing Rules

- Atlas is descriptive; it does not prescribe.
- Atlas stores evidence, not decisions.
- Atlas may contradict itself; contradictions are labelled, not resolved.
- Atlas preserves minority evidence.
- Nothing is deleted; understanding evolves through supersession.
- Every entry carries a permanent identifier.
- Every entry carries a date, an author, a method, and a confidence label.
- Atlas is append-only.

Skeleton ends here.
